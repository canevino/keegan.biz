01

Triennial 2025: The Exchange

Boston Public Art Triennial

Campaign design for the Triennial’s citywide exhibition, adapting the existing identity across print, advertising, wayfinding, and environmental applications.

Working from the Triennial’s established identity, I adapted the More ____ campaign across street posters, A-frames, buses, digital kiosks, event graphics, and exhibition collateral. Some pieces were direct adaptations; others were new compositions built inside the same system. Type, color, and image treatment stay consistent while hierarchy changes with scale, distance, and the amount of information each format has to carry. More Connection carries the campaign at its most information-dense: artwork, wayfinding, map, funder credits, and the exhibition message all have to remain legible at once. It became a useful reference point for how far the system could stretch without losing its identity.

03

Paul Winter Consort

poster series + concert production

Three concert posters built around images with a direct relationship to the music, place, and program.

Three posters for concerts I also helped produce for the Paul Winter Consort. Each is built around source material specific to the program: the Hubble Ultra Deep Field for Charles Ives, a NOAA view of the vernal equinox, and an archival photograph from Crestone, Colorado. The image source sets the structure of each poster, while the typography shifts with the subject.

Adventures in the Universe

A concert at Yale's Woolsey Hall built around Charles Ives, marking his 150th birthday and drawing on his unfinished Universe Symphony. The image is NASA's Hubble Ultra Deep Field. Ives's best-known piece, The Unanswered Question, sets a lone trumpet asking against a quiet string background that never answers, and the Deep Field does something similar as a picture: a small patch of seemingly empty sky that holds thousands of galaxies. The type is heavy and condensed, stacked to fill the width of the sheet against all that empty space.

Spring Equinox Celebration

The image is a NOAA satellite capture of the earth at the vernal equinox, when daylight and darkness are nearly equal at every latitude. I treated the image and set the type curving around the globe, using a serif that points back to the late 60s and 70s, when the Consort formed and made the albums it's still known for.

This Glorious Earth

An Earth Day concert at the Cathedral of St. John the Divine. The photograph comes from the Paul Winter Consort's archive of Crestone, Colorado, the landscape behind the Consort's 2007 album of the same name, recorded at 11,800 feet in the Sangre de Cristo Mountains. Using an image from the Consort’s own archive ties the poster directly to the landscape behind the recording. The type is the same heavy condensed style as Adventures in the Universe, so the two read as a series, but set smaller here to let the photograph carry the poster.

Design: Keegan Andrulis, for the Paul Winter Consort, 2023–2024.
