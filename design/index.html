More ____

Boston Public Art Triennial

Campaign design for the Triennial’s citywide exhibition, adapting the existing identity across print, advertising, wayfinding, and environmental applications.

Campaign system

Working from the Triennial’s established identity, I adapted the More ____ campaign across formats that had to work at very different scales: street posters, event graphics, A-frames, buses, digital kiosks, and exhibition collateral.

Some pieces were direct adaptations of existing campaign layouts; others were new compositions built inside the same system. The typography, color, and image treatment stay consistent while the hierarchy changes with the viewing distance and amount of information.

Anchor piece

More Connection

This poster carries the campaign at its most information-dense: artwork, wayfinding, map, funder credits, and the exhibition message all have to remain legible at once. It became a useful reference point for how far the system could stretch without losing its identity.

02

Better Angels

Boston Public Art Triennial

Identity, collateral, signage, and participatory print for Heather Kapplow’s installation. I led design in collaboration with the artist, Triennial curatorial staff, and production team.

identity + participation

Better Angels was a participatory installation by Heather Kapplow. I led the graphic design, working with Heather, the Triennial’s curatorial staff, and production staff to translate the installation into a coherent visual system across print, signage, staff materials, and visitor takeaways.

I also assisted with fabrication and production of the visual elements. The pieces had to work inside the encounter itself: guiding visitors, supporting staff, gathering responses, and giving people objects they physically handled and carried away.

03

Making Public

Boston Public Art Triennial

Event identity for the Triennial’s convening, carrying one workshop image across posters, wayfinding, programs, and digital communications.

Event identity

Making Public was the Triennial 2025 convening at Roxbury Community College. I built the event system around an image created at a youth community workshop with NUBE East Boston and ñ press, treating it as both source material and a recurring graphic field.

The system alternates between image-led and cream-field layouts, then scales across posters, social graphics, a program, event communications, and a wide digital banner.

A compact typographic system had to hold up across very different proportions while keeping the workshop image visibly present.

01

Two poster modes

The image can fill the page and carry the atmosphere, or sit as a contained object inside a quieter cream composition.

02

Information changes the layout

The program and reverse flyer carry far more copy, so the image becomes a smaller anchor instead of competing with the schedule.

03

Digital versions

Social formats use the same two modes, keeping recognition without forcing every piece into an identical template.

Design: Keegan Andrulis. Source image created at a youth community workshop with NUBE East Boston and ñ press. Produced for the Boston Public Art Triennial.
