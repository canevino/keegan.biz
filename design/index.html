Triennial 2025: The Exchange

01

More ____

Boston Public Art Triennial

Campaign design for the Triennial’s citywide exhibition, adapting the existing identity across print, advertising, wayfinding, and environmental applications.

Working from the Triennial’s established identity, I adapted the More ____ campaign across street posters, A-frames, buses, digital kiosks, event graphics, and exhibition collateral. Some pieces were direct adaptations; others were new compositions built inside the same system. Type, color, and image treatment stay consistent while hierarchy changes with scale, distance, and the amount of information each format has to carry. More Connection carries the campaign at its most information-dense: artwork, wayfinding, map, funder credits, and the exhibition message all have to remain legible at once. It became a useful reference point for how far the system could stretch without losing its identity.

03

Making Public

Print and digital collateral for Making Public, a convening at Roxbury Community College in October 2025. The image came out of a youth workshop that Ñ Press ran with NUBE East Boston, part of Gabriel Sosa's Triennial project with the community print studio. From there it ran across posters, on-site wayfinding, a program handout, a QR flyer, and a newsletter ad for Boston Art Review, each one carrying a different amount of information.

Design and production: Keegan Andrulis. Brand guidelines by Siena Scarff Design. Produced in-house at the Boston Public Art Triennial.

02

Better Angels

Boston Public Art Triennial

Identity, collateral, and signage for Heather Kapplow’s participatory installation, developed in collaboration with the artist.

Better Angels was a participatory installation by Heather Kapplow. I led the graphic design, working with Heather, the Triennial’s curatorial staff, and production staff to translate the installation into a coherent visual system across print, signage, staff materials, and visitor takeaways. I also assisted with fabrication and production of the visual elements.
